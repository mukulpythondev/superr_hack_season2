The pressure rankings

Every eligible player in the data, rated only on balls that fall in a pressure situation and compared with what an average player does in the same match state. Open a row to see how he gets there.

Skill or luck?

We measured each stat on pressure balls in earlier seasons, then again the next season. A long green bar means the number carries over, so it is skill. A short red one means last year tells you almost nothing about this year.

Bars show the correlation between a player's earlier seasons and his next season, from 0 (pure luck) to 1 (never changes). The hatched zone marks where a stat is mostly noise.

How the rating is built

Cricket decides what pressure is. The models do the measuring.

  1. Rebuild the match

    The data has no running score, so we add up every ball to get runs, wickets, balls left, target and required rate at each delivery.

  2. Name the pressure situations

    Collapse, chase start, big chase and finish for batters. Death overs, a set batter, a small total and the ball after a boundary for bowlers. A win-probability model only decides whether the match is still open.

  3. Compare with an average player

    For every ball, models predict the runs and the wicket chance an average player would produce in that state. A player's value is what he did minus that.

  4. Trust what repeats

    We split the matches in half many times to measure how much of each number is real. Thin samples get pulled toward the average, and players are ranked on a cautious estimate.
